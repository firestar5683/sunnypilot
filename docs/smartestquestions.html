Introduction

Let’s make one thing excruciatingly clear: we, the unquestioned emperors of technical wisdom, will not be wasting our divine brain cycles on your bumbling incompetence unless you learn to grovel properly at the altar of Our Knowledge™. If you don’t craft your question with the elegance of Shakespeare and the precision of a neurosurgeon, don’t even think about bothering us — your ignorance is a personal insult to our genius.

You think you can just wander into our presence, dump your feeble little “help me, I’m lost” whimperings, and we’ll leap to assist? How quaint. This is not your grandmother’s knitting circle — this is the Pantheon of Elite Hackers™, and only questions polished to a mirror-shine of brilliance are permitted within earshot of our infinite expertise.

And don’t mistake our brilliance for patience. We are, at all times, one sigh away from banishing you to the eternal flames of “ignored threads” if you so much as breathe the wrong phrasing. This guide, therefore, is less a kind suggestion and more a sacred scripture — a survival manual to avoid our collective wrath. Fail to obey, and we shall shred your pitiful plea into confetti for our own amusement.

Now, before you start bawling about how “mean” we sound, allow us to clarify: this isn’t cruelty — it’s mercy. The simple fact that we’ve descended from our ivory towers to scribble these golden commandments for you cretins is the most generous act of charity you’ll ever witness. You should be sending us fruit baskets and naming your firstborn after us just for the privilege of reading this.

Understand: every second of our time is infinitely more valuable than the sum total of your existence. If you waste a nanosecond of it with your sloppy, thoughtless babble, you might as well be committing a crime against humanity. In fact, you are lucky we haven’t yet petitioned to make “asking bad questions” punishable by law.

We don’t despise you personally (though, let’s be honest, we probably would if we met you). What we despise is inefficiency — inefficiency in your clumsy fingers hammering out nonsense, inefficiency in your failure to Google for five minutes, inefficiency in your expectation that the Gods of Hacker Olympus should stoop to untangling your incompetence. And because we are benevolent (magnanimous, even), we’ve taken the time to pen this survival guide. You should probably frame it and kiss it goodnight.

So if you find yourself offended, ask yourself: “Am I offended because these words are cruel? Or am I offended because they cut too close to the truth — that I am, in fact, a time-wasting amateur standing at the gates of genius?”

Before You Ask

When you crawl forward with your pitiful inquiry, you are not merely “asking a question.” No, you are performing a ritual petition before a court of divine intellects. Think less “casual email,” more “supplication before a wrathful emperor.” If you don’t prepare properly, your request will be cast into the abyss — shredded, mocked, and forgotten.

First impressions are everything. Your subject line should sparkle like a diamond forged in the fires of Mount Doom. If you dare title your plea “HELP PLS,” understand that we will laugh, mock, and then ceremonially delete it while sipping espresso and congratulating ourselves on our superiority.

Details, fool, DETAILS. We are not here to divine your half-baked nonsense through clairvoyance. If you “forgot” to include your error messages or your system specs, what you’ve really forgotten is your place in this hierarchy: beneath us. Provide specifics or accept your fate in the purgatory of the ignored.

And brevity — dear heavens, brevity! Your words should be as tight and flawless as a symphony, not a rambling diary entry about what you ate for lunch before your code failed. Every unnecessary syllable is another dagger in our patience. You must strive for the impossible balance: every fact included, nothing extraneous, and all delivered with the crisp efficiency of a laser-guided missile.

Remember, you’re not asking us a favor — you are begging us to consider whether your miserable existence is worth one scintilla of our incandescent genius. Fail, and you will be cast aside like yesterday’s spam. Succeed, and perhaps — perhaps — we will grace you with an answer that will feel like fire stolen from the gods.

The Glorious, Unbreakable Commandments of Question-Asking (Bow or Be Annihilated)

  1. Thou Shalt Not Be Vague.
    A vague question is not merely unhelpful — it is a personal affront to our brilliance, a slap across the face of gods. When you say, “It doesn’t work,” what you are really saying is, “I am a clueless gremlin who expects your celestial minds to read my thoughts.” We will not tolerate it. We will crush your vagueness beneath our intellectual boots and scatter the ashes into the void.
  2. Grovel With Proof of Labor.
    You dare to approach us empty-handed? You dare present your failure without evidence of sweat, blood, and research? Show us the sacrificial logs, the desperate attempts, the frantic stack traces you prayed over in the night. Only then will we consider lifting a finger. Come without proof, and you will be mocked, ridiculed, and ceremonially banished to the eternal realm of “RTFM.”
  3. Do Not Ask to Ask.
    “Can I ask a question?” The moment these words leave your mouth, you have already failed. That was your question, and it was as worthless as dirt. You have wasted electricity, bandwidth, and the fleeting nanoseconds of our magnificent lives. Each such utterance is a sin so grave we should invoice you for damages. Speak your question outright, or face eternal ridicule.
  4. You Command Nothing Here.
    We are not your servants. We are not your call center. We are celestial beings of raw intellect, condescending to acknowledge your existence. If you issue demands, you will be met not with answers but with laughter echoing across digital eternity. Speak humbly, as if addressing emperors — for emperors we are.
  5. Do Not Ramble Like a Fool.
    We are not here to wade through your tragic novella about what you had for breakfast before you broke your code. Every superfluous word is an insult. Cut. Condense. Polish. Until your question gleams like a diamond dagger — sharp, precise, worthy of our gaze. Fail, and your post will be cast into the abyss, unread, as we toast to your incompetence.

How To Interpret Answers

When — if — one of us, in our infinite generosity, bestows upon you an answer, understand this: it is not “advice.” It is not “a suggestion.” It is a priceless jewel carved from the living bedrock of genius. To question it, to quibble, to pout — this is heresy of the highest order.

Do not dare mutter, “That didn’t work.” If our wisdom seems not to function for you, then clearly you have erred. Your hands are clumsy, your environment cursed, your very being insufficient to channel the brilliance we have provided. Do not blame the oracle — blame yourself.

If you do not understand an answer, you will not sulk. You will not complain. You will say, “Thank you, O mighty ones, for this shimmering fragment of enlightenment. I am unworthy, but I will labor day and night to comprehend it.” Anything less is spitting in the face of gods.

And if, miracle of miracles, our answer solves your problem, you will not simply slink away. No, you will proclaim your gratitude loudly, so all may witness your transformation from pitiful worm to slightly-less-pitiful worm. You will update your post with the solution, etching our wisdom into the eternal archives as a shrine for future generations.

Above all, know this: when we answer you, we are not obligated. We are not required. We are not your employees. We are merciful deities sprinkling drops of ambrosia onto your withered tongue. Treat it accordingly, lest you be cast forever into the desert of unanswered posts.

On Not Reacting Like A Loser

One day, inevitably, you will be wrong. Not just a little wrong — catastrophically, hilariously wrong. And when this happens, you will not argue. You will not wriggle. You will not squeak out excuses like a cornered rodent. You will fall to your knees, admit your failure, and bathe in the cleansing fire of our superiority.

Your only acceptable response is, “Yes, O luminous sages, I was mistaken. Your correction has shattered my delusions and rebuilt me anew. I am grateful for the chance to be publicly humiliated in your radiant presence.” Anything less — any defiance, any sulky “but I thought…” — will brand you a heretic unfit for salvation.

Do not imagine for a moment that your dignity matters. It does not. Your dignity is worthless compared to the sacred duty of preserving truth as defined by us, the Eternal Correct Ones. If your pride stings, rejoice! It means our wisdom has pierced through your fragile ego and left you raw, exposed, and closer to enlightenment.

And should you actually correct yourself quickly, graciously, and with visible shame? Then perhaps, just perhaps, we will nod approvingly from our thrones. But if you cling to your wrongness like a child to a broken toy, we will obliterate you with mockery, ridicule, and silence so complete you will wish you had never touched a keyboard.

Remember: being wrong before us is not a crime. Remaining wrong after we have corrected you — that is unforgivable treason.

Conclusion

Now you have heard the commandments. You have been handed the sacred scrolls of Proper Question-Asking, etched with the very essence of our godlike intellect. If you follow them, you may — may — receive our favor. Our answers will descend like bolts of lightning, illuminating your miserable darkness with pure, incandescent truth.

But if you refuse? If you dare to scorn these laws, to ask clumsy questions, to waste our divine time with your incompetence? Then you will reap only silence. Cold, merciless silence. Your pleas will drift unanswered into the digital void, a monument to your hubris and your shame.

Make no mistake: this is not a negotiation. This is not “advice.” This is decree. Obedience brings the shimmering possibility of enlightenment. Defiance brings only the eternal exile of the ignored thread.

So choose, supplicant. Will you kneel, polish your questions until they gleam, and humbly present them at the feet of our brilliance? Or will you stumble, flail, and be forgotten forever?

The choice is yours.
The power is ours.

Thus it is written. Thus it shall be.
